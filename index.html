✅ Compliance checklist

Two jobs in one document: check the property is legally lettable, and check your own sourcing business is legally allowed to charge a fee for finding it.

Form 02 of 04Per deal + annual reviewEngland & North Lincolnshire
Start here if you are new to sourcing. Section A is about your business, not the property. Taking a sourcing fee without redress scheme membership, ICO registration and AML supervision in place can mean a fine and an unenforceable fee agreement.

A. Your sourcing business — before any fee

  • Property sourcing counts as estate agency work, so membership of a government-approved redress scheme is a legal requirement. Join the Property Redress Scheme or The Property Ombudsman.
  • You are holding investors' ID documents and financial information, so you must register with the Information Commissioner's Office and pay the annual fee.
  • Estate agency businesses must register for AML supervision with HMRC and carry out customer due diligence. Confirm current scope directly with HMRC.
  • If reservation fees pass through your account you need a CMP scheme. The cleanest route for a new sourcer is to never hold client money — funds go direct to the solicitor.
  • Covers you if an investor claims your figures caused them a loss. Check the policy names property sourcing as a covered activity.
  • Signed before you disclose the property address. Use the Fee Agreement Template in this pack and have a solicitor review it.
  • Sole trader or limited company, registered with HMRC, separate business bank account. Sourcing fees are trading income, not capital gains.

B. Article 4 direction

An Article 4 direction removes permitted development rights, so a change from family home to small shared house needs full planning permission rather than happening automatically. This kills HMO conversion deals if you miss it.

  • Check with North Lincolnshire Council directly rather than relying on a map layer or a previous deal in the same street — boundaries can run mid-road.
  • Save the council response or planning portal page as a PDF for the deal file. A screenshot with no date is worthless in a dispute.
  • Application cost, decision timescale, and a realistic view on approval odds. If the deal only works as an HMO and planning is uncertain, say so plainly in the deal pack.
  • If the property is already a lawful HMO, get evidence. A Certificate of Lawfulness is the strongest proof.

C. HMO licensing

  • Mandatory HMO licensing applies across England to properties occupied by five or more people forming two or more households sharing facilities. Confirm the current test before relying on it.
  • Councils can extend licensing to smaller HMOs or all rented property in designated areas. Ask North Lincolnshire Council whether any designation covers this address.
  • Licensed HMOs have minimum sleeping room sizes and required facility ratios. Measure rather than estimate — a room that fails takes a bedroom out of your rent model.
  • Application fee, renewal period, and any works the council requires as a licence condition.
  • Interlinked alarms, fire doors, emergency lighting, escape routes. On a full HMO conversion this is a material cost, not a rounding error.
  • A licence is not planning permission. Both are needed, and one being in place says nothing about the other.

D. EPC and property condition

  • Look it up on the national EPC register. Note the expiry date as well as the band.
  • Rented domestic property in England and Wales currently needs at least band E to be let, subject to exemptions. Tightening has been consulted on, so check the current and announced future requirement before modelling a long hold.
  • Read the EPC's own recommendations. Insulation, heating and glazing upgrades on older terraced stock run to thousands and are often missing from a first-pass refurb figure.
  • Annual gas safety check, and an electrical installation condition report at least every five years. Budget a rewire if the consumer unit and wiring look original.
  • On auction stock or anything with visible movement, damp or roof problems, a survey costs far less than the structural work it finds.

E. Anti-money-laundering file

Keep these for every investor you transact with, in a form you could hand to a supervisor without reconstructing anything.

  • Photo ID plus proof of address. Record what you saw, when, and how you verified it.
  • Identify the beneficial owners behind the company, not just the named director on the Companies House record.
  • Where the money actually came from — not just that it exists. Savings, refinance, sale proceeds, inheritance, third-party investment.
  • Screen against the UK sanctions list and check for politically exposed person status.
  • A short written rationale for low, medium or high risk, and what extra checks a higher rating triggered.
  • Kept securely, consistent with what you told the investor in your privacy notice.

💷 Sourcing fee agreement

A starting structure for the agreement between you and an investor: what you're being paid for, when the fee becomes due, and what happens if the deal falls through.

Form 03 of 04Signed before address disclosureDraft — solicitor review needed
This is a template, not a contract to use as-is. It is not legal advice and has not been drafted for your circumstances. Have a solicitor review and adapt it before you issue it. An agreement that gets the fee trigger or cancellation terms wrong is the single most expensive mistake a new sourcer can make.

Parties

Commercial terms

Clauses

1. What the sourcer is providing

1.1 The Sourcer will identify and introduce property investment opportunities to the Investor that reasonably match the criteria the Investor has confirmed in writing.

1.2 The Sourcer will provide, for each opportunity introduced, the information set out in the deal pack: property details, the financial appraisal and its assumptions, comparable evidence relied on, and known compliance considerations.

1.3 The Sourcer acts as an introducer. The Sourcer does not act as the Investor's agent in negotiating or completing the purchase, and does not handle the Investor's purchase funds.

2. What the sourcer is not providing

2.1 The Sourcer is not providing financial advice, investment advice, tax advice, legal advice, or a valuation or survey of any property.

2.2 Figures in any appraisal are estimates based on assumptions stated at the time. Rental values, resale values, refurbishment costs, interest rates and tax treatment can all change, and actual returns may differ materially from any projection.

2.3 The Investor is responsible for their own due diligence, including survey, legal title review, and verification of all figures, before committing to any purchase.

3. When the fee becomes payable
Decide this deliberately. Fee on exchange is the more defensible position, because the Investor is legally committed at that point. Fee on offer-accepted is more likely to be disputed if the deal later collapses. Pick one and delete the other.

3.1 The sourcing fee of   becomes payable on exchange of contracts on a property introduced by the Sourcer, and is due within   days of exchange.

3.2 Any reservation fee paid at the point of reserving a deal is   (refundable / non-refundable / deducted from the sourcing fee on completion). The circumstances in which it is returned are set out at clause 5.

3.3 Only one sourcing fee is payable per property, regardless of how many parties connected to the Investor complete the purchase.

4. Introduction and non-circumvention

4.1 A property is treated as introduced when the Sourcer first discloses its address to the Investor in writing, unless the Investor can show they were already actively pursuing that specific property and evidences this within   working days of disclosure.

4.2 The Investor will not, for   months from the date of introduction, acquire an introduced property through another party in order to avoid the fee. This includes acquisition by a company, family member, or associate of the Investor.

4.3 The Investor will keep details of introduced properties confidential and will not pass them to third parties without the Sourcer's written consent.

5. Cancellation and refunds

5.1 If the seller withdraws, or the purchase cannot proceed for reasons outside the Investor's control, any reservation fee held is returned within   days and no sourcing fee is payable.

5.2 If the Investor withdraws after reserving a deal, the reservation fee is treated as set out at clause 3.2.

5.3 Where the Investor is an individual entering this agreement outside their business, statutory cancellation rights may apply to contracts agreed away from the Sourcer's premises. Take advice on whether this applies to your arrangement.

6. Liability

6.1 The Sourcer's total liability under this agreement is limited to the sourcing fee actually paid in respect of the property in question.

6.2 Nothing in this agreement limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for anything else that cannot lawfully be limited.

7. Complaints and redress

7.1 Complaints should be made in writing to the Sourcer, who will acknowledge within   working days and respond in full within   working days.

7.2 If the complaint is not resolved, the Investor may refer it to the Sourcer's redress scheme, named above.

8. Data protection

8.1 The Sourcer processes the Investor's personal data, including identity and financial information, to provide these services and meet anti-money-laundering obligations. Handling and retention are set out in the Sourcer's privacy notice.

9. General

9.1 This agreement is governed by the law of England and Wales and those courts have exclusive jurisdiction.

9.2 This agreement is the entire agreement between the parties in respect of its subject matter and replaces any earlier discussion or representation.

9.3 Any variation must be agreed in writing and signed by both parties.
